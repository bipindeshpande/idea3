# Lean Canvas Template

## Overview
The Lean Canvas is a one-page business plan that helps you quickly document and validate your business model. It's perfect for startups who want to focus on what matters most: solving customer problems.

## Why Use Lean Canvas
- **Fast to complete** - One page, not 50
- **Focus on validation** - Emphasizes testing assumptions
- **Visual and shareable** - Easy to discuss with team
- **Iterative** - Update as you learn

## Validation Plan

### Problem Validation
- [ ] Interviewed 10+ potential customers
- [ ] Confirmed problem exists and is urgent
- [ ] Verified customers are actively seeking solutions
- [ ] Tested willingness to pay

### Solution Validation
- [ ] Built MVP with core features
- [ ] Tested with real users
- [ ] Measured key metrics
- [ ] Iterated based on feedback

### Business Model Validation
- [ ] Tested pricing with customers
- [ ] Validated revenue model
- [ ] Confirmed unit economics work
- [ ] Projected path to profitability

## Tips for Success

1. **Be specific** - Vague answers won't help you
2. **Focus on validation** - Test assumptions, don't just document
3. **Update regularly** - Canvas should evolve as you learn
4. **Get feedback** - Share with mentors and advisors
5. **Prioritize** - Focus on riskiest assumptions first

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Filling it out once and never updating
- Being too generic or vague
- Not validating assumptions
- Focusing on features instead of problems
- Ignoring key metrics
- Overestimating unfair advantages

---

## Lean Canvas Checklist

- [ ] Problem section based on customer interviews
- [ ] Customer segments are specific and reachable
- [ ] Value proposition is clear and testable
- [ ] Solution addresses validated problems
- [ ] Channels are realistic and cost-effective
- [ ] Revenue streams are validated
- [ ] Cost structure is realistic
- [ ] Key metrics are measurable
- [ ] Unfair advantage is defensible
- [ ] Validation plan is actionable

---

*Remember: The Lean Canvas is a living document. Update it regularly as you validate assumptions and learn from customers.*
